**OOXML Toolkit 0.3.0** opens **OpenDocument files** (`.odt`, `.ods`, `.odp` and friends) alongside Office files, and fixes several ways an edit could be lost on save.

![Animated tour](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/DominikPalo/OOXML-Toolkit/main/docs/demo.gif)

## What's new

- **OpenDocument support.** ODF packages are ZIP + XML like Office files, but have no relationships or content types. They now open as first-class documents:
  - The **overview** shows the document properties from `meta.xml` — title, author, keywords, dates, editing time, custom properties and statistics.
  - A **Manifest** view of `META-INF/manifest.xml` replaces the (empty) relationship view, marks missing and unlisted files, and part headers show the media type from the manifest.
  - The **package check** verifies `mimetype` (first entry, stored, no trailing whitespace, matches the manifest) and compares the manifest with the parts. Damaged or unusual packages are reported instead of failing to open.
  - Adding or renaming a part updates the manifest, and `mimetype` is always saved first and uncompressed. (Deflating it breaks ODF consumers.)
- **Fixed: edits made while saving.** Changes made during a save are no longer lost or shown as saved, and a part restored with undo during a save keeps its place in the archive.
- **Fixed: Inspector drafts.** Text typed into an Inspector field is committed before you save or close, instead of being left out of the file or discarded.
- **Fixed: concurrent writes.** Two quick saves to the same file can no longer interleave.
- **Fixed: part names in `[Content_Types].xml`.** Names are percent-encoded and decoded. Renaming a part with a space in its name no longer writes an invalid override, and files that already use encoded names no longer get false “no content type” findings.

Good to know: there are no slide, text or cell previews for OpenDocument yet — you see and edit the XML. Encrypted ODF entries are listed but cannot be read.

### First launch — the builds are not notarized yet

- **macOS:** drag the app from the `.dmg` to *Applications* and open it once — macOS blocks it. Then open
  **System Settings → Privacy & Security**, scroll down and click **Open Anyway** next to
  “OOXML Toolkit was blocked”. (macOS 14 and earlier: right-click the app → **Open** instead.)
  From a terminal, this does the same: `xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine "/Applications/OOXML Toolkit.app"`.
  Notarization needs a paid Apple Developer ID; until then this one-time step is required.
- **Windows:** SmartScreen may warn about an unrecognised app — choose **More info → Run anyway**.
- **Linux:** `chmod +x OOXML-Toolkit-0.3.0-linux-x86_64.AppImage && ./OOXML-Toolkit-0.3.0-linux-x86_64.AppImage`.
  If it does not start, install FUSE 2 (`libfuse2` / `fuse2`).

## About OOXML Toolkit

View, edit and compare Office Open XML packages (`.docx`, `.xlsx`, `.pptx` and friends) and OpenDocument packages right down to the XML: a tree of every part and element, source editor and inspector, worksheet / document / slide previews, text / regex / XPath search, side-by-side comparison, bookmarks and history, and a package check. See the [README](https://github.com/DominikPalo/OOXML-Toolkit#readme) and the [user guide](https://github.com/DominikPalo/OOXML-Toolkit/blob/main/docs/guide.md).

## Known limitations

- Password-protected Office files and legacy binary formats (`.doc`, `.xls`, `.ppt`) cannot be opened.
- OpenDocument files have no slide, document or worksheet preview yet.
- The package check verifies structure, not ECMA-376 schema validity.
- The slide and worksheet previews are simplified (no fonts/fills/charts; raw cell values).
- Builds are not notarized (see above) and there is no auto-update yet.
