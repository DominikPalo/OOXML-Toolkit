Patch release: the **comparison view keeps the part you are looking at**, **renaming a part keeps its relationships**, and password-protected files get a clear message.

## Fixed

- **Comparison options no longer jump to another part.** Toggling **Ignore formatting-only differences** or
  **Ignore attribute order** — or clicking **Refresh** or **Swap sides** — re-runs the comparison. It used to
  select the first changed part and collapse the tree; now the part you were viewing stays open and the
  expanded folders are kept.
- **Renaming a part keeps its relationships.** The part's `.rels` file used to stay behind under the old
  name. It now moves with the part, and relationship targets pointing to and from the part are rewritten
  (matched case-insensitively; fragments, absolute targets and external links are kept). A rename that
  would overwrite an existing `.rels` file is refused before anything changes, and the whole rename is a
  single undo step.

## Changed

- **Password-protected and legacy Office files.** These are OLE compound files, not ZIP archives, so they
  still cannot be opened — but instead of a generic “Not a ZIP archive”, the error now says the file looks
  like a password-protected or legacy binary Office file (`.doc`, `.xls`, `.ppt`).

### First launch — the builds are not notarized yet

- **macOS:** drag the app from the `.dmg` to *Applications* and open it once — macOS blocks it. Then open
  **System Settings → Privacy & Security**, scroll down and click **Open Anyway** next to
  “OOXML Toolkit was blocked”. (macOS 14 and earlier: right-click the app → **Open** instead.)
  From a terminal, this does the same: `xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine "/Applications/OOXML Toolkit.app"`.
  Notarization needs a paid Apple Developer ID; until then this one-time step is required.
- **Windows:** SmartScreen may warn about an unrecognised app — choose **More info → Run anyway**.
- **Linux:** `chmod +x OOXML-Toolkit-0.4.1-linux-x86_64.AppImage && ./OOXML-Toolkit-0.4.1-linux-x86_64.AppImage`.
  If it does not start, install FUSE 2 (`libfuse2` / `fuse2`).

## About OOXML Toolkit

View, edit and compare Office Open XML packages (`.docx`, `.xlsx`, `.pptx` and friends) and OpenDocument packages right down to the XML: a tree of every part and element, source editor and inspector, worksheet / document / slide previews, text / regex / XPath search, side-by-side comparison, bookmarks and history, and a package check. Everything in [0.4.0](https://github.com/DominikPalo/OOXML-Toolkit/releases/tag/v0.4.0) is included — see the [README](https://github.com/DominikPalo/OOXML-Toolkit#readme) and the [user guide](https://github.com/DominikPalo/OOXML-Toolkit/blob/main/docs/guide.md).

![Animated tour](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/DominikPalo/OOXML-Toolkit/main/docs/demo.gif)

## Known limitations

- Password-protected Office files and legacy binary formats (`.doc`, `.xls`, `.ppt`) cannot be opened.
- OpenDocument files have no slide, document or worksheet preview yet.
- The package check verifies structure, not ECMA-376 schema validity.
- The slide and worksheet previews are simplified (no fonts/fills/charts; raw cell values).
- Builds are not notarized (see above) and there is no auto-update yet.
